Cloudbet: proposed 2027 structure

Incumbent global betting partner

Cloudbet holds rights across every event but cannot legally activate in most of the markets where those events land.

The fix Strip out what they cannot use, replace it with inventory that travels, and reprice against what is actually deliverable.
1

Retitle to reflect scope

Naming sets the hierarchy

  • Official International Betting Partner of PFL — international feed and out-of-territory digital only.
  • Regional partners take Official Betting Partner of PFL in [market].
  • No global betting exclusive. Category exclusivity becomes territorial by definition.
2

Event assets: geo-targeted virtual overlay only

No physical branding out of territory

  • Cage mat, apron and LED delivered by digital board replacement on the international feed.
  • Domestic feed carries the regional partner. Neither brand appears in the other's market.
3

Year-round global content, not event-tagged

The core of their reach

Owned series running across the calendar and attached to no specific event, so nothing is tied to a fight they cannot legally promote.

  • Fighter predictions series.
  • Behind-the-scenes and fighter access content.
  • Archive and highlights licence for their own channels.
  • VIP hospitality allocation at events across the slate, including events outside their territory list.

Access to ambassadors, rest of world. Fighters for shoots, appearances, social content and odds or predictions segments, including fighters from or popular in the markets where they are licensed. Ambassadors travel where signage cannot, so this is the strongest asset in a package with no in-venue presence. Booked against an agreed number of days and allocated to avoid clashes with regional partners.

4

In-territory uplift

Full domestic package where they are licensed

  • For any event falling inside their agreed territory list, Cloudbet takes all physical branding, in-venue, hospitality and ticketing, plus domestic social and digital.
  • Priced on a rate card by market tier, so expansion is a purchase rather than a renegotiation.
  • Gives them a direct commercial reason to widen their licensing footprint.
5

Netflix blockbuster build-up, as an upweight

Paid add-on, not part of the base

  • Rest-of-world rights to fight week, weigh-in, press conference, fighter content and owned social around the four Netflix events.
  • Domestic slice sold separately to the US partner.
  • This is the cheapest lever we have for holding the fee up once the territory list narrows.

Confirm Netflix advertising standards on gambling, and specifically whether they will accept an operator unlicensed in the US anywhere near the property, before this is priced.

6

Slim down the territory list

Reserved markets go to licensed partners

  • Remove all regulated European markets — UK, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Netherlands.
  • Remove the United States and Australia.
  • These form the reserved market schedule, sold to licensed regional partners.
  • PFL retains the right to add markets to the schedule on notice as new events are announced.
7

First refusal on new territories

Keeps the relationship growing

As Cloudbet acquires licences, they take first refusal on that market at the pre-agreed rate, moving it from the standard package to the in-territory uplift.

Alt

The all-in exclusive

Sole betting operator across the full slate, at a premium

If Cloudbet would rather hold everything than accept a narrowed territory, we sell it. But it is priced as the closure of every other betting sale on the slate, not as an uplift on the current fee.

  • Exclusive betting operator in cage and ring at all events across the slate.
  • Broadcast branding on every feed where it can lawfully be carried.
  • All social and digital betting inventory, with no regional operator alongside them.
  • Full ambassador and VIP hospitality allocation rather than a rest-of-world share.
  • Netflix build-up included in the base rather than sold as an upweight.
  • First refusal on the 2028 slate.

The floor. Total forecast value of the regional deals plus the standard package sales, plus an exclusivity premium for the optionality we give up. Below that number this is a worse outcome than the split structure, so the floor is set before we open rather than negotiated toward.

Single season, with a 2028 option. A multi-year all-in locks us out of the regional market entirely, and we would have no partner relationships built if the model underperforms.

Two conditions. Where an operator cannot lawfully be advertised, that inventory comes off the domestic feed rather than passing to Cloudbet: exclusivity means nobody else buys it, not that they appear where they legally cannot. And the deal carries activation minimums in the markets they can serve, with a release if unmet, so the biggest betting markets do not go dark for a season.

Regional partners lead wherever events land

Licensed operators, full domestic rights, one per market

  • United States 16 eventsLargest single line. Sportsbook, with first refusal on real-money iCasino in the seven live states.
  • United Kingdom 4 eventsSold standalone. Do not bundle into a pan-European package.
  • France anchor eventLyon anchors the European MMA run. Pipeline already active.
  • DACH, Iberia, ItalySold against the remaining European MMA slots as venues confirm.
  • AustraliaNo events, but real MMA handle. Broadcast and digital led.
  • Russia fill marketNo events, but among the strongest viewership regions for the sport and a deep Russian and North Caucasus contingent on the MMA roster, including current champions. Domestic betting runs through locally licensed bookmakers, so treat this as a standard package sale rather than a full domestic deal, and clear sanctions and payment routes with legal before pitching.
  • Middle East 2 eventsNot a betting market. Sell locally to non-endemic categories and keep betting creative off the domestic feed.
  • What a regional partner gets: all physical branding and in-venue, VIP hospitality and ticketing, domestic broadcast, and domestic social and digital.
  • Category exclusivity is territorial by definition. No partner holds a global betting exclusive. This is the clause that has been causing the fragmentation.

Fill the rest with one standard package

Same asset set every time, rate card by market tier

Operators outside the reserved markets buy a fixed shape of deal built only from inventory that travels. This is the structure the incumbent global partner moves onto, and it becomes the template for every deal in this tier.

  • Geo-targeted virtual overlay on the international feed. No physical branding at any event outside their territory list.
  • Access to PFL content across the full event cycle: pre-event build-up, fight week, in-event and post-event material, plus archive and highlights for their own channels.
  • VIP hospitality at events across the slate, including events outside their territory list. Allocated as a fixed number of seats per event for customer and VIP programmes.
  • Access to regional ambassadors. Fighters from or popular in their market, available for shoots, appearances, social content and odds or predictions segments. Ambassadors travel where signage cannot, so this is usually the highest-converting part of the package for an operator with no in-venue presence. Allocated per partner to avoid clashes and booked against an agreed number of days.
  • In-territory uplift. If an event lands inside their market, they take the full domestic package, priced on the rate card rather than renegotiated.
  • Netflix blockbuster build-up available as a paid add-on, not included in the base.
  • First refusal on new territories as they acquire licences.

Split the US by category, not just by market

Three separate sales, non-conflicting

  • Sportsbook — roughly 38 states. The anchor US deal.
  • Prediction markets — all 50 states, which brings California, Texas and Georgia into play. Official data and integrity co-operation are part of the paid package, not a courtesy.
  • Social casino — sell it, but on a twelve-month term with a hard regulatory-change break. Seven states banned the model during 2026.

These stay non-conflicting only if the non-sportsbook categories are held to digital, social and free-to-play. No cage, no ring, no broadcast bug.

Content partnerships need Watch & Bet flexibility

The change that unlocks a whole tier of deals

Wildz and Sportsbet type deals are content and streaming led rather than signage led, and they are the natural fill for markets where we will never sell a full domestic package. The IMG Arena Watch & Bet agreement currently gates who can stream PFL, which is why these conversations keep stalling.

  • What we need: an express right to sub-licence streaming to operators we sign directly.

Watch & Bet returns 70% of gross on content. Model the sponsorship upside against any revenue share we give up before opening the negotiation.

Timing

Working back from US budget cycles

  • Now to October 2026 — settle the global partner position, publish the reserved market schedule and category definitions internally, open the Watch & Bet conversation.
  • October to December 2026 — US sportsbook and US prediction markets. US operators set 2027 budgets between September and November, so this window is fixed.
  • November 2026 to February 2027 — UK, France, DACH, Iberia, Australia.
  • Q1 2027 — social casino, content partnerships and affiliate fill.
